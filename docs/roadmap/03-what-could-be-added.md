## 3. What could be added

From what the Switch versions and other tools offer:

| What | Why | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Navel Rock and Birth Island** (Lugia, Ho-Oh, Deoxys) as chapters of the AutoStory | The Switch versions put the Mystic Ticket and the Aurora Ticket in the bag after the Hall of Fame; on the Game Boy Advance they were given out at events only. | Medium: two ferry destinations, two caves, the triangle puzzle. Made (experimental) and run on a console: all three were caught, each with the Ultra Balls that two trips to Lady Gillian buy. A wild Ho-Oh takes several tries (about 2 balls in 100 catch it; a failed try loads the save from before the battle), Deoxys is thrown at at full HP because any hit makes it faint, and Lugia has no text box before its battle. |
| **A complete Pokédex** as a goal | Pokémon HOME 4.1.0 (October 2026) takes whole boxes from the Switch versions, one way, and gives a special Celebi in the mobile app for a complete FRLG Pokédex. The AutoStory's Pokédex collection catches what the grass, the caves, the water and the Safari Zone have, and reads the PC's boxes to see which species are still missing. For the rest it has segments of its own, all experimental: evolving by a stone (also the stones the shop does not sell, fetched where the game has them), by a level (trained at Victory Road, or by rounds of the Elite Four with the Exp. Share on the Pokémon) and by friendship (walked with on the Indigo Plateau, counted by the program); the four trades; the eggs of the Four Island Day Care for the baby Pokémon; Tyrogue steered to Hitmontop or Hitmonchan with vitamins; Dratini by Super Rod fishing in the Safari Zone; Unown (the puzzle of the Tanoby Key); and Porygon (coins from the Game Corner, read from the screen). A place that fails twice in a row is left out for the rest of the run. On a console the table reached 163 of 175 species; later runs caught Ho-Oh, Lugia, Deoxys and Unown and went on to the Porygon route and the vitamins, which were still being tested. Omanyte and Omastar cannot be had in this save (the Helix Fossil is gone once the Dome Fossil is taken), so 173 of 175 is the most. | Large: needs the next row too |
| **Trading between two Switches** (local wireless): trade evolutions (Alakazam, Machamp, Golem, Gengar), the other version's Pokémon, the other starters | The only way to the Pokémon one cartridge cannot get. Pokémon Automation can drive two consoles at once. | Large, and needs two consoles and two games |
| **Egg hatcher and breeder** (Four Island day care) | Pokémon Automation has these for Sword/Shield, Brilliant Diamond/Shining Pearl and Scarlet/Violet; for FRLG only an Egg RNG tool in development. The AutoStory's Day Care stage (experimental, see the row above) does the part the Pokédex needs. | Medium |
| **The fanfare skip** in the existing farmers and shiny hunters | See above. | Small |
| **A target search for Starter, Static and Gift RNG when the SID is not known** (taken, not wired in) | Taken on 25 November 2026 from dayfine's fork (MIT), branch `frlg/rng-plan`: `PokemonFRLG_RngTargetSearch` finds Target Seed and Advances for a trainer the way the Searcher and Initial Seed tabs of [Ten Lines](https://lincoln-lm.github.io/ten-lines) do, `PokemonFRLG_RngPlan` orders the targets to ask for when SID Helper has narrowed the SID down to a few weighted candidates, and `PokemonFRLG_RngPlanTracker` says what to do after each result (shiny, hit but not shiny, missed). New files with unit tests, and two accessors on `SeedsDatabase`; none of the existing RNG programs changed, and no program uses them yet, so nothing shows in the program. Not taken: the same fork's change that makes Gift RNG's loop callable from its AutoStory (it rewrites `PokemonFRLG_GiftRng.cpp`, which has changed here since, and is only useful with that AutoStory). Next step if wanted: an option "find the target myself" in Starter RNG, which needs a console to test. | Medium |
| **Counts per species** in the hunts of wild Pokémon, and a clip in the AutoStory and the RNG programs | pokebot-gen3 (for emulators) reports encounters, "phases" and milestones to Discord. Pokémon Automation counts species in its other games (`EncounterFrequencies`), and FRLG has a reader of the species name (`WildEncounterReader`, tested on stored pictures) that no program calls yet. A hunt would read the name once per encounter and add it to the table of the message. Not done: it needs the text reader in the loop of a hunt, which cannot be tried without a console, and the clip in the AutoStory (the battle code goes by seconds, and a clip takes a few to save) and the RNG programs. | Medium |
| **A status page for the phone** on the home network (what runs, the last screenshot, the stats) | **Done** (issue #13): the agent serves it at `/` and `/status`: whether the bot runs, the screen every ten seconds, today's counters, the matches of the League, the switches that are on and the end of the log, in a browser with the token typed once. It only looks. Tested in the agent's own tests (the page's policy, its script, the counters) and looked at in Chrome at desktop and phone size; not yet on a real Pi. pokebot-gen3 has an HTTP API; Pokémon Automation's latest code has the start of an HTTP server. | Medium |
| **Nugget Bridge as the story's source of money** | The Rocket at the end of Nugget Bridge gives a Nugget (sold for 5,000) every time until he is beaten, and a lost battle costs 16 or 24 per level of the party's highest Pokémon at that point of the story. Pokémon Automation's farmer makes about 100 Nuggets in two hours; twenty are 100,000, and in the run of 13 November money cut the story's purchases short. Needs a party that loses (the others in the PC) and selling at a Mart. | Medium |
| **A second sign of a shiny**, next to its sound | The AutoStory and Pokémon Automation's FRLG hunts of wild Pokémon go by the sound alone, and say so when no sound comes in. AutoController2 also times the start of the battle, which a Pokémon with Intimidate or a slow picture sets off. A sign from the picture would be better: the sparkles, or the colours of the species against its two palettes. | Medium |
| **The AutoStory's own screen readers tested on stored pictures** in every build | **Partly done** (issue #16). The ball mark of a caught species and the two HP bars of a battle are tested on the 27 pictures of the upstream test repository that show them (the readers are declared in `PokemonFRLG_AutoStoryBattle.h` now, the tests are `PokemonFRLG_AutoStoryReaders_Tests.cpp`, 121 tests in every build): the right answer on the pictures as they are; on each picture re-colored 10 ways (limited and expanded range, 15% and 30% darker, brighter, two gammas, warm, cool, green tint) the right answer or -1 ("not sure"), never a wrong one; and on pictures with something painted over them (a bar that is half full, covered, or full after an empty part; a mark that is not clear; a box that is not cream), because the repository has no picture of a damaged Pokémon. Mutation-tested: of 12 changes to the readers' thresholds and boxes, 11 fail the tests (the twelfth, one game pixel shift of the mark area, is read the same). It found two weaknesses, not changed because nothing here can try a change on a console: the player's bar reads 1.0 on the first frame of an encounter in front of a sand coloured backdrop (there is no player's box yet; the program reads it only at the menus), and under a green or warm tint it reads 1.0 on some other first frames as well. The load menu (is there a save, its POKéDEX count) and the naming screen have no picture in the test repository: they need captures from a console ([OTHERS.md](../../SerialPrograms/Source/ClaudeGG/OTHERS.md)). | Small |
| **Other consoles as outputs: Xbox 360, PlayStation 3 and the original Xbox first, then the retro consoles** | **The PlayStation 3: started, experimental** (27 November 2026). The firmware of the boards turned out to be Pokémon Automation's own, given out only as files to flash (free for non-commercial use, no source), so no mode can be added to it. But the ESP32-S3's NS1: Wired Controller is a HORIPAD S, a plain USB gamepad whose buttons come in the order of a PlayStation pad, and a PlayStation 3 takes such pads. The setup offers the PlayStation 3 with the ESP32-S3 only, with steps of its own, and the console has the list "PlayStation 3 games" with a Claude Plays that names the PlayStation's buttons (README, "PlayStation 3"). Not tried on a PlayStation 3: whether the console and its games take the pad, whether the buttons are where the program thinks, and whether HOME opens the PS menu. **The original Xbox: started the same day, experimental**: it takes only its own controller (XID), so the ESP32-S3 goes into a Raspberry Pi Pico with [OGX-Mini](https://github.com/wiredopposite/OGX-Mini) (MIT for its wired builds), whose list of controllers has the HORIPAD S and whose first mode is the original Xbox; the list "Original Xbox games" has a Claude Plays with the Xbox's buttons, pressed as OGX-Mini passes them on (README, "Original Xbox"). Not tried on an Xbox. The rest of the tiles are dimmed under "Coming soon" (README, "Console"); nothing can be controlled on them yet. For those, firmware of this project's own (GP2040-CE as the base, on an RP2040 that talks to the computer over UART) would be needed. The way: the same kind of board as now (an RP2040 or an ESP32-S3) shows itself to the console as that console's own controller. Two open projects do exactly that: [OGX-Mini](https://github.com/wiredopposite/OGX-Mini) (RP2040; modes for the original Xbox, Xbox 360 (XInput), PlayStation 3, PlayStation Classic and others; its wired firmware is MIT, the Bluetooth part has a mixed license) and its fork OGX-Mini-2026 (more modes; its license was not checked), and [GP2040-CE](https://github.com/OpenStickCommunity/GP2040-CE) (MIT; PS3, Switch, XInput, with no native PS4, PS5 or Xbox One/Series because those consoles authenticate their pads). The MIT code of the first and the last could be used with its notice kept, which GPL projects such as pokebot-gen3 do not allow. What it takes here: the firmware for the boards lives in the separate Packages repository, not in this one, so a mode for each console is built there first; then a controller type and class for each pad (an Xbox 360 or PS3 pad has analog triggers and other buttons than the Switch's: its own state, its own commands), the scheduler that sends them, and the setup's console step lighting the tile up (the comment above `CONSOLES` in `ClaudeGG_Setup.cpp` lists the places). **No games**: no script or program for a game of the original Xbox, the Xbox 360 or the PlayStation 3 was found (the closest are for later consoles or the PC: an Elden Ring script in JavaScript for Xbox One X, Series X and PS4, [AutomaticGamepad](https://github.com/tylearymf/AutomaticGamepad); a Forza Horizon AFK tool for the PC that goes through the ViGEmBus driver, [fh6-afk](https://github.com/hypoxic127/fh6-afk)), so the first programs for such a console would be Claude Plays (it only needs the buttons) and what the user asks for. **The Wii U: started 28 November 2026, experimental, testers wanted.** No board: a Raspberry Pi with a 5 GHz Wi-Fi adapter is its GamePad, through [Vanilla](https://github.com/vanilla-wiiu/vanilla) (GPL-2.0) and the bridge in WiiU/vanilla-bridge, which gives the GamePad's screen as pictures and takes buttons, sticks and touches; the list Nintendo Wii U has Connect the GamePad and a Claude Plays with a touch tool. Next: try it on a Wii U, and the TV picture from the capture card next to the GamePad's. **The retro consoles (PlayStation 1 and 2, GameCube, Nintendo 64, Dreamcast): started 27 November 2026, experimental.** They need their own signal in the controller port, not USB, but [BlueRetro](https://github.com/darthcloud/BlueRetro) (Apache-2.0, an ESP32 in that port) already makes it from a Bluetooth pad. So the wireless boards, which play a Switch Pro Controller over Bluetooth, pair with a BlueRetro adapter; the setup has one tile "Retro consoles" for them, and each console has a list with a Claude Plays by its own buttons, pressed as BlueRetro maps them (README, "Retro consoles"). Not tried: above all whether the board pairs with BlueRetro. **The Xbox 360 (modified, OGX-Mini in XInput mode), the PS4 and PS5 and the Xbox One and Series (through Remote Play on the computer, the board as its USB gamepad, the stream as the video through OBS) and the iPhone (the board as a keyboard, with Full Keyboard Access): started the same day, experimental** (README, "Xbox 360", "Remote Play", "iPhone"). **The PlayStation Classic (OGX-Mini in its PlayStation Classic mode): started the same day, experimental** (README, "PlayStation Classic"). Left under "Coming soon": the Wii U (a Raspberry Pi as its GamePad, with Vanilla: a draft of its own). | Large |
| **Macros on every console: record, save and play back** | **Done, experimental** (28 November 2026, PR #53). Pokémon Automation had Record Keyboard Controller and Turbo Macro, but only in the Switch's list. The program Macro (`ClaudeGG_Macro.h`) is in the list of every console with only Claude Plays, and in Other Games. It records the keyboard or a controller of the computer, saves the macro under a name in `UserSettings/Macros`, and plays it with rounds, a pause and a speed, or plays a typed-in list. It shows the steps by the console's own button names. A goal picture (the screen saved at a moment) lets playing stop as soon as the screen looks like it, also in the middle of a round: the video is checked every 50 ms. The iPhone records and plays its keys (`ClaudeGG_KeyboardState.h`; Pokémon Automation's keyboard passed nothing on to a recording). Not for the Wii U (its own list, with a Raspberry Pi as the GamePad). Not tried on a console yet. | Small |
| **PlayStation 4 and 5 as outputs (open, parked)** | Looked at on 25 November, with nothing built. The consoles authenticate their pads, so a board of our own cannot just be a controller to them (the GP2040-CE project says as much). Routes found: the Titan Two (scripts in GPC with GTuner IV on Windows; it needs a real controller of the console for the authentication; no documented PC interface was found, which the Titan One has in its ConsoleTuner API); the Brook Wingman P5 and FGC converters (no documented way to feed them from a program); [paaas](https://github.com/jfedor2/paaas), a proof of concept in which a Pico plays the pad and passes the console's authentication challenge over the network to a computer holding a real controller (open code, but firmware of our own, and a real controller must stay attached); and everything built on Remote Play (PS4Macro, PS4RemotePlayInterceptor, pyremoteplay under the GPLv3, chiaki-ng under the AGPL, so only as a separate program). GIMX is older and aimed at the PS3 and PS4, gamepad-bridge only translates between controllers, and Ghostpad needs a jailbroken console. None of it is a clean way for a program like ours. | Large |

Left out on purpose: automating online battles on the Switch (Pokémon Champions, released in April 2026, is built around them). That is unfair to the people on the other side. (The one exception is the GO Battle League program of the phone, see "A phone as a console" below.)

pokebot-gen3 is under the GPL, which Pokémon Automation's license terms do not accept, so only its ideas can be used, not its code.
