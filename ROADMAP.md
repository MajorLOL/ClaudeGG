# Claude GG: roadmap

What Claude GG has, what could make the FRLG AutoStory faster, what could be added and what could be modernized. The aim: games on every console that a program can watch and press the buttons of, starting with the Nintendo Switch. What is still open is also on the [issues page](https://github.com/MajorLOL/ClaudeGG/issues).

## 1. What Claude GG has

- Its own name (`ClaudeGG.exe`), icon, logo and "About" window, which has a button that looks for a newer build, only when it is pressed. The links to Pokémon Automation's Discord and its check for a new version are gone. What keeps its old name: see "The name" in section 8.
- A new interface: see [README.md](README.md).
- Several installations side by side, each a process of its own with its own folder, settings, capture card and microcontroller, and an overview with a miniature of each. Each can be given a name of your own and be closed from the overview. On Windows the program is one window: the overview of the installations is the main window, and each installation is a process underneath it whose window opens inside the main window (no window of its own on the taskbar); closing the main window closes them, and `--standalone` starts a program with windows of its own as before. Not yet: this on macOS and Linux (a window of another process can only be a child of a window on Windows), and the keyboard focus of an installation inside the main window is only tried, not proven. See [ClaudeGG_Instances.h](SerialPrograms/Source/ClaudeGG/ClaudeGG_Instances.h).
- A phone as a controller target, first step: the Raspberry Pi bot for Pokémon GO ([pokemon-go-automation](https://github.com/MajorLOL/pokemon-go-automation)) is part of this repository with its history, in [Mobile](Mobile). Its [agent](Mobile/agent) is a small program for the Pi that gives the picture of the phone and takes taps, swipes and keys (over ADB), and Claude GG has the other end of that connection ([ClaudeGG_Mobile.h](SerialPrograms/Source/ClaudeGG/ClaudeGG_Mobile.h)), tested in every build against the agent with a phone that is not there. The setup offers the phone as a console (a third tile) with a page for its Raspberry Pi: address, port, token and a test of the connection. The game "Pokémon GO" has seven programs that start, follow and stop the bot on the Pi (the agent's `/v1/bot`), and each console shows only its own games. Not yet: Claude GG doing the thinking itself in C++ (see "A phone as a console" below).
- Colored text of the programs (written for a white background) that reads on Night and on Day.
- A search in the Output window, with a filter for warnings and errors, and in the settings pages (Ctrl+F).
- Pictures of the screen made without `QVideoFrame::toImage()`, which made the memory grow on some computers: [VideoFrameConversion.h](SerialPrograms/Source/CommonFramework/VideoPipeline/Backends/VideoFrameConversion.h). Not measured on a console: whether a long hunt with the stream history on now stays flat.
- The FRLG hunts for a shiny save a clip of the last seconds when they find one and can send it. Not tried on a console.
- The AutoStory says at the end of a run where its time went: the segments, the goes that did not come through and the longest chapters.
- Pokémon Automation's `main` merged in, with gamepad support (`PA_SDL`, on in the builds).
- 21 bug fixes from a review of the whole code, of Pokémon Automation's open issues and of what goes wrong in other controller programs: [FIXES.md](SerialPrograms/Source/ClaudeGG/FIXES.md).
- A faster build (Ninja and ccache): about 4 minutes when little changed, 35 minutes from nothing.
- A security review: [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). Fixed after it: secrets sealed in the settings file, paths and tokens cleaned from what goes to Claude, firmware checked against hashes, a build that only reads, and the Discord bot out of the Windows build.
- A test of the interface in every build, on a machine that never ran it. A build that fails is not published.

Not tested yet: the AutoStory on this new base (the test run with Charmander is on the branch `frlg-autostory` of the old repository, MajorLOL/arduino-source).

## 2. Can the AutoStory match a speedrunner?

Not today, and not by a small change. The numbers:

| | Time |
|---|---|
| World record, Any% Glitchless (pokeguy, with Squirtle) | 1 h 59 min 34 s |
| AutoStory, second playthrough with Charmander, new game to the Hall of Fame (measured from the logs of 8 to 13 November, 34 runs; the Champion fell on 13 November at 01:49) | 56.9 hours of play |
| of which training levels in the grass (11 segments) | 45.8 hours |
| of which the route itself: walking, talking, the trainers on the way, the gyms, shopping, retries after a failure | 11.2 hours |
| of which the Elite Four and the Champion, each beaten at the first try | 0.9 hours |

So a whole story takes 57 hours, some 29 times the record. Four fifths of that is training:

| Training for | Hours | Levels asked for |
|---|---|---|
| Brock (Routes 1 and 2) | 3.3 | Charmander 18 |
| The rival in Cerulean | 1.3 | 25 |
| Misty | 4.3 | 34 |
| Erika (with Electrode and Vaporeon) | 8.3 | Charizard 36, Electrode 25, Vaporeon 30 |
| Koga | 4.4 | 50, 33, 35 |
| Sabrina (with Lapras) | 1.7 | 47, Lapras 32 |
| Blaine | 7.0 | 52, the team 42 |
| Giovanni | 5.6 | 66, the team 47 |
| The League | 9.8 (11.3 in the first playthrough) | 74, the team 58 |

A speedrunner does not train at all. The published routes ([Squirtle](https://www.speedrun.com/pkmnfrlg/guide/rps95), [Charmander](https://www.speedrun.com/pkmnfrlg/guides/wardh)) take their levels from the trainers on the way and a handful of Rare Candies, and win the hard fights with items bought on the 5th floor of the Celadon Department Store (the Charmander route: 22 X Special, 5 X Defend, 1 to 3 X Speed, 2 X Accuracy, 3 Guard Spec). The Charmander route meets Erika at level 34, Giovanni in Silph Co. at 43 and leaves Fuchsia at 48. They also reset the run when a fight goes wrong; the program cannot count on luck, so it levels until the fight is safe.

What would bring the time down, in the order of what it gains for the work it takes:

1. **X items instead of levels.** Buy the route's X items in Celadon, use them at the start of the gym leaders', Giovanni's, the rival's and the League's fights, and ask for the route's levels instead of today's. Save before each of those fights (the program does); after a loss, try again, and only after a second loss train three levels. Estimate: 5 to 10 hours of training left instead of 46.
2. **Only the lead trains.** Electrode, Vaporeon and Lapras are there for the League's match-ups. With X items the lead can carry the League; the team then only needs to exist for Surf, Strength and Fly.
3. **Running by default.** The option "Running (experimental)" halves the walking time on the maps that allow it. It needs a run from start to end without getting lost before it can be the default.
4. **The Switch versions' fanfare skip.** Opening and closing the help menu with L at the right moment skips a fanfare (2.68 seconds each, up to a minute and a half in a run, found by iamClemi in March 2026). Small, but free once the timing is known.
5. **A leaner route.** Leave out the trainers and item detours the speedrun routes leave out, and replace screen checks by fixed timing where a mistake cannot happen.

With 1 to 3 the story should come under 20 hours; with 4 and 5 perhaps under 12. A separate speed mode that walks by the clock, like a tool-assisted run, could come near 3 or 4 hours, but every step of it would have to be tested on the console, and it would stop at the first dropped button press. The record itself is the best of thousands of human attempts with the fastest starter; a program that has to finish every time will stay behind it.

All of these change how the story plays, so each needs test runs. The first step, the X items, is the one to start with.

### A phone as a console: the plan

The aim, in the words of the request: *in the setup's list of consoles a phone ("Mobile") is added, and the Raspberry Pi is a microcontroller next to the Pico and the ESP32.* For a Switch, Claude GG looks through a capture card and presses through a board; for a phone the Pi is both, over the network, and **Claude GG does the thinking in C++**, as for every console. The Python bot that came with the repository thinks on the Pi (a Pi 4 with 64-bit OS, LiteRT models from RegiBot, OCR) and stays in [Mobile](Mobile) until Claude GG does what it does.

1. **Done: the way to the phone.** The [agent](Mobile/agent) on the Pi and its client in Claude GG, with the protocol in its README and a test in every build.
2. **Done: the setup.** The phone is the third tile on the screen with the consoles. It has no board to pick: the Raspberry Pi is the microcontroller, and a page of its own comes in place of the boards, with the steps (USB debugging on the phone, `git clone` and the installer on the Pi), the address, the port, the token and "Test the connection", which says what phone it found. The token is kept apart from the settings, sealed for the Windows account like the assistant's keys. Left for it: showing a picture of the phone in that test. Issue #28.
3. **Done: the picture.** `GET /v1/video` of the agent is a stream instead of one screenshot at a time (a screenshot takes 1 to 3 seconds): `screenrecord` through `ffmpeg` on the Pi, about ten JPEG pictures a second, one pipeline for all who look. `MobileVideo` in Claude GG keeps the newest picture. Tested in every build with a fake picture; the real pipeline (H.264 through ffmpeg) is tested against a fake `adb` in the agent's own tests; not yet seen on a real phone. Issue #29.
4. **The screen readers in C++:** the three models of [RegiBot](https://github.com/Juancavr6/RegiBot) (MIT; which screen, where the Pokémon, stops and buttons are, the throw's ring) run with a runtime that works on Windows (LiteRT or ONNX Runtime; Pokémon Automation already uses ONNX Runtime for other readers), plus the text reading the bot does with Tesseract. Issue #30. **Started:** seven parts of the Battle League are ported to C++ and held, in every build, to what the Python code answers (the golden files are made by `Mobile/tools/make_*_golden.py`, a test of Python says when one is old): the text side (`battle_nav.py`: which screen it is, where to tap, what a question or a result says; `ClaudeGG_PokemonGoNav`, 6331 answers on 109 screens), the brain of the fights (`battle_engine.py`: damage, matchups, shields, which charged attack to throw, when to switch; `ClaudeGG_PokemonGoBattle`, 14,688 answers), the state of a fight (`battle_fight.py`: 60 fights, 14,913 calls; `ClaudeGG_PokemonGoFight`) and the readers of the picture (`battle_vision.py`: name boxes, Poké Balls, shields, HP bars, ready buttons, the banner, the countdown, the buttons of the screens around a fight; `ClaudeGG_PokemonGoVision` with OpenCV, 696 pictures) and the little game of a charged attack (`battle_minigame.py`: where the type icons are on a picture and which swipes to make over a stream or a row of them; `ClaudeGG_PokemonGoMinigame`, 370 pictures and 243 games of 26,212 steps; the Python side was made deterministic first, as NumPy leaves the sense of the main axis of a group of icons to its linear algebra library). Also ported: the models that read the game (`ClaudeGG_PokemonGoModels`): the screen classifier, the map, the clickable-object and the encounter detector and the throw predictor, run with the ONNX Runtime that the program already has (`Mobile/tools/convert_models_to_onnx.py` makes ONNX copies of the TFLite files and the build checks them against the originals), with the same picture preparation, anchor decoding and non-maximum suppression as `gamebot.py`; held, in every build, to what the TFLite models say on 110 pictures, 18 made-up answers of a network (with many equal certainties, so that the order of equal ones counts) and 200 throws; the encounter detector ends in an operation that ONNX has no name for, so its network is cut in front of it and the post-processing (box decoding, best class, the fast non-maximum suppression of TensorFlow Lite) is done in C++ (`Mobile/tools/make_models_golden.py`; the files are fetched from RegiBot and checked against their SHA-256, they are not in the repository). The loop that ties it together is ported too (`fight_battle` of `gamebot.py`; `ClaudeGG_PokemonGoLoop`): the live picture (or screenshots when there is none), the finger that taps the fast attack on its own and sweeps over the little game, the reader of the banner and the names that runs next to it, the questions (*Exit the Trainer Battle?*, the shield question), giving up and the result. It is held, in every build, to 51 fights that the Python loop played with a fake clock and a fake phone (`Mobile/tools/make_loop_golden.py`: the fights of its tests and seven of its own, where a number of the loop has its limit; the numbers themselves are held to those of `gamebot.py` as well): the C++ loop is shown the same pictures and text, in the same order, and must make the same taps, swipes, log lines and sleeps at the same moments (30,009 events, no difference), and the check of whether the map is in view is held to the Python answer on every test picture. No program uses them yet. The text reader is ported too (`ClaudeGG_PokemonGoText`): it calls the `tesseract` program as `gamebot.py` does (the reader inside the program, tesseractPA, gives text but not the place of the words, and the screens are read by their places) and makes lines of its table; held, in every build, to what `gamebot.py` makes of 354 tables (`Mobile/tools/make_text_golden.py`: 36 read from frames of real fights, the rest made up and random), and on 120 real readings the same words came out at the same places. The phone side of the loop is there too (`ClaudeGG_PokemonGoPhone`): `AgentHost` is the loop's `Host` over the agent of the Pi - the live picture (`/v1/video`, made 704 x 880 as in `gamebot.py`, each picture with the moment it came in), the screenshot on the 1768 x 2208 canvas, the fingers (a press, a quick tap, finger down and up as two events - for that `/v1/tap` of the agent takes a `kind`, so the agent on the Pi has to be updated - and swipes in commands of 50, on the real screen with the rounding of `spin_stop.to_real`), the text reader and the files of what the bot learns (`battle_seen.json`, `battle_minigame.json`, in the format of `gamebot.py`). It is tested in every build, against the agent with a made-up phone (the touches arrive as the loop asks for them, a phone that is off is told in the log, the pictures of the video come with their time) and without one (the pictures, the coordinates, the files). The walk around a fight is ported too (`ClaudeGG_PokemonGoFlow`: `battle_open_menu`, `_battle_to_map` and `battle_run` of `gamebot.py`, with `_settled`, `screen_changed` and the label of a button): from the map or the Battle screen to the party screen of the league (the main menu, the claim of a new rank and the screens after it, the gold reward tiles of a finished set, a new set that has to be scrolled to, the welcome of a new season, the list of leagues, pop-ups with a green button that only goes on, never one that buys, leaves or gives something away), back to the map without ever pressing BACK in a running battle, and a run of battles one after the other. ### The Box Namer (build 5)

An ACE code for these games is a list of box names: the game is made to run them as instructions, so one wrong character is a wrong code. The program takes the list the way the ACE notes publish it (`Box  6: _ F o H I o o r [ FoHIoor]`) or as plain names, and does for each box what a person does: NAME in the box's menu, the name on the naming screen, OK, on to the next box.

What it does to be sure of every character:

- After every character it reads the name back from the screen and compares it with the game's own font, pixel by pixel: the characters typed so far, nothing after them, and the place the next one goes to (which is what tells a space). A character only counts when it is exactly its shape: `c` and `o` differ in one pixel, `.` and `,` in two.
- A space in the eighth place looks the same as an empty eighth place. It is typed over a character that can be seen, which is read first.
- Nothing is pressed blind where a missed or a doubled press would do harm: the arrow of the box's menu is seen on NAME before A, OK is pressed only on a name that reads as written, and the box is seen to change after RIGHT.
- After OK it looks at the box's title, which must show the name. The Switch versions pass a name through the console's list of words that are not allowed when OK is pressed, and do not take one that is on it: the box keeps the name it had, and nothing on the naming screen says so. That is in the game's source ([pret/pokefirered](https://github.com/pret/pokefirered), `MainState_PressedOKButton` in the revision for the Switch), and the [ACE notes](https://pomeg-letterbombers.github.io/pokemon-ace-notes/frlg-faq/) list codes that cannot be entered on the Switch versions for that reason.

What was tested, all of it off the console:

| What | On what | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Reading the name being typed | A capture of this fork's test Switch (the rival's naming screen with AAAAAAA) | Read right, with the place of the next character. |
| | The same capture scaled down to 1280 x 720, the size of this capture card's video, in five ways | Read right each time. |

## 7. Can a program of our own beat the world records?

The records:

| | Time | How |
|---|---|---|
| Best run by a person, Any% Glitchless | 1 h 59 min 34 s | pokeguy, on a Game Boy Player with Game Boy Interface. The leaderboard lists GBA, Game Boy Player, GBI and DS; the Switch versions are not on it. |
| Tool-assisted run | 1 h 44 min 17 s | mkdasher, 2013, on the emulator VBA-rr: 373,735 frames of input, with heavy manipulation of the game's random numbers (Squirtle, later a Clefable with Metronome). |

**An official record: no.** speedrun.com's rules say that "the game itself must not be altered or controlled with outside tools" and that turbo controllers "or such with non-native functions" are not allowed. A program pressing the buttons is exactly that. TASVideos publishes input files made on emulators; a run on a console is not one of those.

**The tool-assisted time: no.** That run is perfect to the frame for an hour and three quarters, made with save states and a view into the game's memory. A program that looks at a capture card cannot get there.

**Faster than the best person, unofficially: possible in principle, far away in practice.** What speaks for it:

- A long tool-assisted run has been played back on real hardware: a Pokémon Crystal run of more than 600,000 frames on a GameCube with Game Boy Interface (it lost its place once, after 32 minutes, through the drift of the game's clock, and that was fixed).
- On a console that asks a USB controller for its buttons, as the Switch does, timed playback worked in short stretches when the playback counted the console's video frames (a Wii U and an RP2040 board); over the air it was off by up to two frames.
- Pokémon Automation's RNG programs hit one chosen moment on the retail Switch versions after calibrating: the seed is set by when a button is pressed on the title screen, and its guide's example is 8 advances late and one seed off at the first try, on target at the next.
- A program does not get tired. Where people play thousands of attempts for one lucky run, it can play twelve two-hour attempts a day and keep the best, and it can do what the published route says it leaves out ("not optimized for RNG manipulation").

What stands in the way:

- The gap between a perfect run and the best person is only 15 minutes in two hours. The AutoStory is not near that kind of play: it looks at the screen after nearly everything it does (a walk of four tiles in the grass takes 3.3 seconds, of which one second is walking), it trains for 46 hours, and it takes Charmander.
- It would be another program, not a faster AutoStory: the speedrun route (Squirtle, no training, X items, Rare Candies, Repels, the bicycle), played by the clock in stretches of timed presses, looking at the video only where the game gives a clear sign to find its place again.
- The Switch versions run the game in Nintendo's own emulator: its timing has to be measured, nothing of the Game Boy Advance runs can be assumed.
- Every stretch has to be tested on the console, and a run stops at the first press that falls a frame late.

The order to go about it, each step useful by itself:

1. **Measure** (build 4): the Input Timing Test tells how much the delay between a press and its effect varies on this controller board, console and capture card, and whether a press can be aimed at a frame. Without that, nothing by the clock can be planned.
2. **Take the waits out of the AutoStory** where the game gives a sign: "Faster battles" (build 4), "Shorter looks in the grass" (build 5, see below), running.
3. **X items instead of levels** (section 2): from 57 hours to under 20.
4. **A speedrun mode** for one stretch first, for instance from the bedroom to the first rival battle: timed presses, the video as the check. Compare with the same stretch in a record run's video.
5. **The whole route** stretch by stretch, then the random numbers (the starter's stats from the title screen, as Pokémon Automation's Starter RNG does), then many attempts.

A fair guess: steps 1 to 3 are weeks, and bring a story that plays in less than a day. Steps 4 and 5 are months of test runs, with a first whole run somewhere around 3 to 4 hours. Coming under 1 h 59 min 34 s would take much more than that again, and may not be reachable through a capture card at all; the Input Timing Test is the first thing that can tell.

One thing would be new either way: no program-played run of FireRed or LeafGreen on a console turned up in this search. The first one that finishes at speedrun pace has no time to beat.

### What the logs of the test runs already tell (13 November)

The Input Timing Test has not run yet. Three things can be read from the logs of the AutoStory itself.

**Where the time of a run goes.** The run of 12 to 13 November (9.4 hours: six hours of training for the League, the Elite Four and the Champion, then 26 tries at Zapdos) counted its own time by activity:

| Activity | Minutes | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Battles (415), and reading text, moves and species names | 309 | 55% |
| Walking the route | 92 | 16% |
| Walks along the grass while training (1,614) | 85 | 15% |
| Menus, healing, saving, resets, flying, single steps | 42 | 7% |
| The rest | 36 | 6% |

**The look after a walk in the grass.** After every walk along the grass the program watched 1.5 seconds for a battle to start. Of the 1,614 walks, 1,266 ended without one and waited the whole time: 32 minutes of the run. Of the 348 battles, the video saw 343 start during the walk itself; that look found the other 5, 1.3 seconds after the walk. The game decides on a wild battle in the frame a step ends, and what follows changes the whole picture every frame until the screen is black (two fades to gray and back, then the transition). So a screen that stays the same for 0.3 seconds has no battle starting. **Build 5, experimental:** the option "Shorter looks in the grass" of the AutoStory and the Level Trainer ends the look then. About one second less for each walk without a battle: 23 minutes of that run. With "Faster battles" (about 5 seconds for each battle that is not a catch, some 30 minutes of that run) the two would have taken a little under an hour off the 9.4, if they work as meant; neither has run on a console.

**Walking by the clock.** A walk is one held press, timed to the tile (16 frames a tile), and the video counts the tiles after it. In the logs of 8 to 13 November the video could count 15,009 of them:

| | Walks | Exactly the tiles asked for | Short | A tile too far |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Along the grass (up to 4 tiles) | 7,330 | 7,329 | 1 | 0 |
| On the route (1 to 41 tiles) | 7,679 | 7,464 | 215 | 0 |

The short ones are not the clock. 100 of the 215 are walks over rock stairs: the steps in front of the Indigo Plateau and the two ends of Nugget Bridge. On those tiles the game itself walks slower, 23 frames a tile instead of 16 (`PlayerIsMovingOnRockStairs` in its source). A walk over four of them came out two tiles short 83 times of 85 and one tile short the other two times; a walk over two of them one tile short, 14 times of 14. One stair tile fits in the spare time of a press (19 walks of 20). The program sees the missing tiles on the video and walks them. A clock that counts the stairs would not have to, and the script's generator knows where they are (it does not let the player run there). Another 22 are one walk in the Power Plant, two tiles short all 22 times: the Max Repel wears off on it, and the game shows "REPEL's effect wore off…" and takes the text away again by itself because a direction is held. And 79 are walks that did not start at all, 58 of them single steps and most of those at the same four places; those have not been looked into. Along the grass a timed walk came out right 7,329 times of 7,330, and no walk went a tile too far.

Between two walks the program takes 0.8 seconds (the median of 6,973 walks that were followed straight by another): it lets the last step end, counts the tiles on the video and waits for a still picture. The game needs up to a quarter of a second of that. In these logs the pauses add up to 87 minutes, next to 163 minutes of held presses.

So for step 4, walking by the clock is not what stands in the way on this controller board and console: the clock has to count the rock stairs and the steps of a Repel, and it can chain the presses where the program now stops to look. What a speed mode has to solve is what is not walking: more than half of a run is battles, and the texts, menus and fades between them have no fixed length yet. That is what the Input Timing Test and "Faster battles" are for.

## 8. Claude in the program (build 13)

Build 13 brings Claude, Anthropic's AI model, into the program, on the user's own API key. The guide is [ASSISTANT.md](SerialPrograms/Source/ClaudeGG/ASSISTANT.md). In short:

- **A chat in a panel of the main window.** It answers questions about the programs, with tools that look things up instead of sending everything along: the program list, the open program's options and the log come from the program itself, the source from GitHub, a part at a time.
- **Building and fixing.** A change is kept in the conversation until the user has seen it and clicks "Commit". It goes to a branch `claude/<topic>`, never to `main`, and into a pull request the user creates. A pull request is now built and tested like every build, and Claude can read the result and the compiler's errors.
- **Claude Plays** (experimental), for FireRed and LeafGreen: Claude plays toward a goal the user writes down. To save tokens it gets what the program reads from the screen instead of a picture wherever that is enough, and a whole battle, scene or walk is one call to a routine of the AutoStory.
- **Claude Plays in every list, and the list Other Games** (build 18). The session (the notes, the conversation that starts over, the limits) is one piece of code for all games now (`ClaudeGG/ClaudePlays`), and FireRed and LeafGreen run on it too. For a game without routines of its own, Claude gets tools that work anywhere: a picture of the screen or of a part of it at twice the detail, the text on the screen as the program's OCR reads it (a fraction of a picture's tokens), buttons pressed, buttons and sticks held, a button mashed, and a wait until the picture is still or changes. After every action the program says how much of the picture changed and whether it still moves, which tells whether a press did anything for a few tokens. Each list's Claude Plays knows its game's name and controls; Sword and Shield, Brilliant Diamond and Shining Pearl, Legends: Arceus, Scarlet and Violet and Legends: Z-A also bring their own routine to save the game, when the user allows saving. Let's Go is played with its single Joy-Con.
- **New games: Octopath Traveler 0, Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, Splatoon 3, Animal Crossing: New Horizons.** There is no program for any of the four, so none has a tile of its own on the page with the games: each is a choice of the option "Game" in the Claude Plays of Other Games, which tells Claude the game's name and buttons. (Until 26 November 2026 each had a tile of its own, made by `GameOnlyList`, which is still in the code. A game that gets programs gets a folder in `VideoGames/` and a list of its own, with Claude Plays on top.) **Standing rule: every game that is added gets Claude Plays**, with a profile in `ClaudePlays_Games.cpp` whose notes say only what is certain (buttons from the game's manual or two sources that agree; nothing about the game's assists or modes that was not checked). The four were added from the controls pages of the games' guides, not from a console: none of it has run on one. Splatoon 3 can also be aimed by the controller's motion, which the program cannot make, so Claude is told to aim with the right stick. There is no program for any of the four yet. Ideas that are open and not started: a Tableturf Battle player for Splatoon 3 (the only one found is [AutoTableTurf](https://github.com/fga401/AutoTableTurf), GPL and Python: its ideas could be rebuilt, its code could not be taken), AFK farms for Animal Crossing's clock-bound chores, and repeated races for Mario Kart's unlocks.
- **The name.** The program was called PocketPilot, then PocketPilot Studio, and has been Claude GG since build 98: `ClaudeGG.exe`, its log `ClaudeGG.log` and its settings `ClaudeGG-Settings.json` (the first start copies a `SerialPrograms-Settings.json` that an older build left, and leaves the old file; the files of Claude's panel with the old name are moved to the new one); the window, the About window, the zip (`ClaudeGG-Windows.zip`) and the titles of the releases carry the new name. The icon is one set of files (`IconResource/claudegg.*`). After build 103 the branch (`main`) and the source folder (`ClaudeGG`) were renamed too, and the builds became versions: Claude GG V 0.1.0.0, 0.1.0.1 and so on (tags `v0.1.0.N`). Kept on purpose: the folder `SerialPrograms/` of the code (renaming its ~3000 files would make every merge of Pokémon Automation's `main` a conflict) and the secret that seals the keys (a new one would make the keys on a computer unreadable).

How far it was tested: every build holds a conversation with a made-up Claude and a made-up GitHub, up to a commit on a branch of its own, plays three turns as Claude Plays does, plays a whole session of the Claude Plays of every list on a made-up console (since build 18), and checks what needs no connection (the lines "Assistant test" of the build's log). The build machine has no key: nothing has run against the real Claude API, no real commit was made, and Claude Plays has not run on a console, in any game.

What could come next:

- **Seeing the game in the chat: done** (issue #17). The tool `look_at_game` hands the chat a picture of what a console of the open program shows, the whole screen or a part of it at twice the detail, and says why there is none when there is no video. The picture checks (which part, which size) run in every build, and so does a call through the window's own hook; it has not been used with a real capture card or a phone yet.
- **Playing without API credit: done** (issue #18). Every Claude Plays, FireRed and LeafGreen included, has an option "Who plays". With "Claude Code plays" the program starts a server on 127.0.0.1 (the port is an option, 28417 as it comes) in the form Claude Code connects to (MCP, Streamable HTTP, the revisions 2024-11-05 to 2025-11-25) and offers the game's tools and `done`; the Claude is the user's own, on a subscription, so the program needs no key and pays nothing. The server is its own code (`Assistant_McpServer`), not Pokémon Automation's HTTP server of [#1879](https://github.com/PokemonAutomation/Arduino-Source/pull/1879), which was not looked into. Only this computer can reach it, and only with a token: every request must carry the token the program made once (32 random bytes, kept encrypted in a file of its own), and its Host and Origin must be this computer's, so that a web page that is open in the user's browser cannot use the server (DNS rebinding). The command that adds the server to Claude Code, with the token, is written to `ClaudeGG-Mcp-Connect.txt` in the settings folder, not to the log. The session ends when Claude Code calls `done`, at the limit on actions, after the minutes without a call that are set, or when the program is stopped (a call that is queued then is not run, and the client is told). What every build tests: 109 checks of the protocol, the HTTP side and the token (18 mutations of that code were tried by hand: 17 are caught, one is equivalent), and a whole session through a real socket on a console that is made up (the refusals, the handshake, a look as a JPEG, a press, a hold, `done`, and the port closed afterwards). Not tried: a real Claude Code on a subscription (the Claude Code that runs here cannot sign in), a console, and a game. What was tried: Claude Code's own `claude mcp add` and `claude mcp list` (connected with the right token, refused with a wrong one) and the official MCP SDK for Python as a client.
- **The routines of the other games as tools: started for Legends: Z-A** (issue #19). Since build 18 every game has Claude Plays, but only FireRed and LeafGreen bring routines for whole battles, scenes and walks. What is done: in Legends: Z-A the result of every action also says what the program's own detectors recognize (the main menu, the white dialog box, the transparent dialog box of a battle, the blue message box): "Screen: still now. The program sees: the main menu.", and Claude is told what that is worth (a hint, not instead of the picture). Only the detectors that have stored pictures in the test repository are used, because those are the ones whose answer is known: the words are held to the answers of the detectors' own tests on 25 of those pictures in every build (a picture a detector recognizes has its phrase, one it does not has not; they also run on pictures re-colored like other capture cards), and the words for every mix of answers are tested. What is not done: the other games (the detectors of Sword and Shield, Scarlet and Violet, Legends: Arceus and Brilliant Diamond and Shining Pearl have too few stored pictures, or need more of the program around them, to say how far they can be trusted), and the routines themselves as tools (a whole battle, flying on the map): each needs a game to try it on. Which ones first is best decided by what a real session spends its tokens on. The picture tests could not be run here (the detectors need the whole program and the test repository, which only the build has); the words and the line of the result were run here on a made-up console.
- **A game that is not played in English: done for the text reader** (issue #20). Claude Plays has an option "Language of the text" (English, Japanese, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Korean, Chinese): the tool `read_text` reads the screen in that language, and Claude is told which it is (a line of its instructions, checked for every game in every build). Claude itself reads the pictures it asks for in any language, and the chat can look at the game too (`look_at_game`). What stays English: the routines of FireRed and LeafGreen in the AutoStory read move names, the opponent and the battle text as English, and the OCR needs the language data of the language (the option says when it is not there). Dutch has no OCR data in Pokémon Automation; not tried with a game in another language.
